# Khmer copy review

> Generated by `node scripts/generate-khmer-review.mjs`. Do not edit by hand —
> change `packages/localization/src/messages/km.ts` and regenerate.

**This is a release blocker.** Every Khmer string below was written to be correct
and idiomatic, but none has been checked by a native speaker. No automated test
can tell us whether the wording sounds natural to a Cambodian shop owner, or
whether the tone is right for asking a neighbour about money.

## What to look for

1. **Does it sound like a person?** These are read by shopkeepers, not accountants.
2. **Is the tone right?** Reminder messages especially must feel polite and
   unembarrassing. Nothing here should ever pressure or shame a customer.
3. **Is the vocabulary the one merchants actually use** for credit and repayment?
4. **Is anything ambiguous** out of context, as a button label with no sentence
   around it?

## Please do NOT "fix" these

Khmer has no grammatical plural, so keys ending `_one` and `_other` are
**intentionally identical**. English needs two forms ("1 customer" / "3
customers"); Khmer uses one. Making them differ would be wrong.

`KHQR`, `CSV` and `PDF` are proper nouns and stay in Latin script.

## Messages sent to customers — review these first

These are the only strings a **customer** ever reads. The merchant sees the
message, may edit it, and chooses whether to send it — the app never sends
anything on its own. They must feel polite and must never embarrass anyone.

Shown filled in with a realistic example so the tone can be judged as sent.
